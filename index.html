About

Ekambaranathar and Elavarkuzhali

In this sacred temple, the presiding deity is worshipped not as a stone Lingam, but as a Prithvi Lingam formed from holy earth. Goddess Parvati, revered here as Elavarkuzhali, performed deep penance beneath the sacred mango tree in Kanchipuram. She fashioned a Lingam from earth and worshipped Lord Shiva with devotion.

When the floods of the Vegavathi River threatened to destroy the Lingam, the Goddess protected it by embracing it with her divine form. Pleased with her extraordinary devotion, Lord Shiva granted her His divine darshan and bestowed His sacred grace. In later times, this holy place became a centre of worship for sages and devotees. It was expanded by the Pallava, Chola, Pandya, and Vijayanagara dynasties, evolving into the magnificent Sri Ekambaranathar Temple seen today.

Temple Speciality

Pancha Bhoota Sthalam – Earth

Ekambaranathar Temple is one of the five Pancha Bhoota Sthalams, representing the Earth (Prithvi) element, where Lord Shiva is worshipped as the sacred Prithvi Lingam.

Sacred Mango Tree (Sthala Vriksham)

The ancient mango tree inside the temple is believed to be over 3,500 years old, bearing four different varieties of mangoes, symbolizing the four Vedas.

Aayiram Kaal Mandapam

The famous Thousand-Pillar Mandapam displays fine Vijayanagara architecture, with long rows of beautifully carved stone pillars forming a grand ceremonial hall.

Sahasra Lingam (1008 Lingams in One)

A rare Sahasra Lingam inside the temple depicts 1,008 Shiva Lingams sculpted on a single Lingam, symbolizing the infinite forms of Lord Shiva.

Nilathingal Thundam Perumal Shrine

Within the temple complex is the shrine of Nilathingal Thundam Perumal, a Divya Desam of Lord Vishnu, making Ekambaranathar Temple a rare site where both Saiva and Vaishnava traditions coexist harmoniously.

Sunlight Illumination (Surya Olirchi)

A unique phenomenon occurs when direct sunlight falls on the main deity. On Rathasaptami day (around February), the sunlight directly illuminates the Prithvi Lingam, creating a spectacular divine display that attracts devotees and spiritual seekers alike.

Sundarar’s Eye-Restoring Miracle

Sundarar, one of the revered Nalvar saints, lost his eyesight as a divine consequence and later regained it through the grace of Lord Ekambareswarar. This temple is celebrated as the sacred place where Sundarar’s left eye was miraculously restored, making it a site of devotion and wonder.

Temple Highlights

Temple History & Antiquity

The temple has existed since at least the 6th century CE, during the Pallava period, and was later expanded by the Chola, Pandya, and Vijayanagara dynasties, making it one of South India’s most continuously worshipped Shiva temples.

Lingams Around the Temple

The temple corridors and prakarams are lined with numerous Shiva Lingams, reflecting the deep Saivite heritage and continuous tradition of worship.

Vikata Chakra Vinayagar

This rare form of Lord Vinayaka, known as “Vikata Chakra”, is authenticated through Sthala Purana and Agama traditions. Worshipped here, he is considered a divine representation that bridges Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions, symbolizing the unity of spiritual paths.

Rati–Manmadan Sculptures

On two pillars of the temple, there are sculptures of Rati and Manmadan: one sitting on a parrot and the other on a honeybird. These exquisite carvings represent divine love and reflect the artistic excellence of the Vijayanagara period, showcasing intricate craftsmanship and symbolic meaning.

Naalvar Paadal Petra Sthalam

This temple is one of the Nalvar Paadal Petra Sthalams, praised in the hymns of Sambandar, Appar, Sundarar, and Manikkavacakar. It reflects not only spiritual significance but also the continuity of Tamil literary and devotional tradition.

192-Foot Rajagopuram

The majestic southern Rajagopuram rises to about 192 feet (59 metres) with 11 tiers, and stands among the tallest temple towers in South India, built during the Vijayanagara era.

Sivan Parvathi

On the first pillar in the second praharam of this temple, there is a sculpture of the Lord seated in a meditative position, with Ambika standing beside him and covering the Lords right eye with his left hand. Under it are the figures of Nandi Munivars.
